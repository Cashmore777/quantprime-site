## Module 4: Managing Risk

Your lot size determines how much money you put on the line per trade. You need to pick a percentage of your account that you're willing to comfortably risk on every trade.

Get comfortable with calculating your lot size quickly and under pressure, because time and efficiency equals better results.

What are pips? A pip is a measurement — just like centimeters or meters. We always want to look at the fourth and fifth number after the decimal point. That's how you calculate pips.

On TradingView, your long or short position tool will show you the amount of pips that your stop loss is placed at, so you can avoid doing the calculation manually.

Managing your trade: If you want, you can exit some of your trade at a certain percentage of the full take profit to secure some profits. This is called taking partials.

If you're going to take partials, please make sure that you put your stop loss to break even so that the trade becomes risk-free. You've secured some profit and you've taken your risk off the table. Now, whatever happens — you win.

Backtesting is the art of looking at previous days and finding your strategy actually playing out. This is where you find information, learn lessons, and develop rules about your trading strategy — without losing any real money.

## Module 7: Chart Structure

The basics of the chart at a fundamental level.

Consolidation is zigzag price action with no real direction up or down. It just zigzags between two points — a high point and a low point of the range it's currently set inside of.

These windows can hold great opportunities if you understand the flow of how the chart moves across different periods of time. You can catch ping-pong style trades up and down throughout the range. Or you can be patient, wait for a liquidity sweep in one direction, and then place a trade in the other direction once there's confirmation.

Trends: Trending price action is when price is moving in one direction. This happens after a consolidation has its false breakout — it'll go in the opposite direction.

Manipulation normally happens in one direction, and then the trend pushes in the opposite. The trend continues until balance has been achieved, and then price can begin to consolidate all over again.

Volatile conditions: When the market is volatile, choppy, and hard to read — it's best to stay out completely. This can be caused by global events or certain economies having a shift. High volatility is usually on high-impact news days, or when Trump opens his mouth.

The balance of liquidity: After liquidity has been grabbed, price will push towards a fair value gap to rebalance itself. The underlying cycle is imbalance, balance, imbalance, balance.

## Module 9: Candles

There are a few different types of candles you need to look out for: bullish, bullish engulfing, bearish, bearish engulfing, and doji candles.

A bullish candle is a candle that closes above where it opened. A bullish engulfing candle is a candle that closes above the body and wick of the candle that was previously created. Bullish candles mean we're likely to go up.

Bearish candle characteristics are the same as bullish, but the opposite way around. A bearish candle closes below where it opened. A bearish engulfing candle closes below the body and wick of the previous candle. Bearish candles mean we're likely to go down.

Doji candles are different. This is a candle that creates a top wick and/or bottom wick, then closes exactly where it started — with no body, just wicks.

These candles indicate a lack of volume, but they can also indicate a reversal is coming soon. It's best not to trade around these. If you see one, just stay out — it's not a guaranteed direction.
